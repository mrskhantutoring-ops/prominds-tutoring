Algebra 1

Solving equations

Solve: 3(x − 4) + 5 = 2x + 7

Show the worked solution

Distribute the 3 to both terms: 3x − 12 + 5 = 2x + 7

Combine on the left: 3x − 7 = 2x + 7

Subtract 2x from both sides: x − 7 = 7

Add 7 to both sides: x = 14

Check: 3(14 − 4) + 5 = 35, and 2(14) + 7 = 35. ✓

Where students get stuck: distributing 3 to the x but forgetting the −4.

Inequalities

Solve: −2x + 6 > 14

Show the worked solution

Subtract 6 from both sides: −2x > 8

Divide both sides by −2 — and flip the inequality sign, because we divided by a negative:

x < −4

Check with x = −5: −2(−5) + 6 = 16 > 14. ✓

Where students get stuck: the flip. It is the single most-missed step in Algebra 1 inequalities.

Systems of equations

Two numbers add to 34. The larger number is 4 more than twice the smaller. Find both numbers.

Show the worked solution

Let the smaller number be s and the larger be l.

From the words: s + l = 34, and l = 2s + 4

Substitute: s + (2s + 4) = 34 → 3s + 4 = 34 → s = 10

Then l = 2(10) + 4 = 24. The numbers are 10 and 24.

Check: 10 + 24 = 34, and 24 is 4 more than twice 10. ✓

Where students get stuck: turning the words into the second equation.

Slope & graphing

For the line 3x − 2y = 12, find the slope and y-intercept — then find y when x = 6.

Show the worked solution

Solve for y: −2y = −3x + 12

Divide everything by −2: y = (3/2)x − 6

Slope = 3/2, y-intercept = −6

When x = 6: y = (3/2)(6) − 6 = 9 − 6 = 3

Where students get stuck: the slope comes out a fraction, and dividing by a negative flips every sign.

Algebra 2

Quadratics

Solve: x² − 5x − 14 = 0

Show the worked solution

We need two numbers that multiply to −14 and add to −5: those are −7 and +2.

Factor: (x − 7)(x + 2) = 0

Set each factor to zero: x = 7  or  x = −2

Check x = 7: 49 − 35 − 14 = 0. ✓

Where students get stuck: picking the factor pair — the signs have to work for both the product and the sum.

Exponentials

A bacteria culture starts with 500 cells and doubles every 3 hours. Write a function for the count after t hours, and find the count after 12 hours.

Show the worked solution

Start with the initial amount, times the growth factor raised to the number of doubling periods:

f(t) = 500 · 2t/3 — the exponent is t/3 because a doubling happens every 3 hours, not every hour.

After 12 hours: f(12) = 500 · 24 = 500 · 16 = 8,000 cells

Where students get stuck: writing the exponent as 3t instead of t/3.
